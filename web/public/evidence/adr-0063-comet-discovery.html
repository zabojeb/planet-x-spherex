0063 Comet discovery: a comet search over any sequence and a known-comet watch

  • Status: Accepted (extends 0048, 0049, 0054, 0060)
  • Date: 2026-11-15

Context

The solar-system tools (ADR-0060) analyse comets that are already known: a co-moving sequence from IRSA MOST, Afrho, CO2/CO bands. Finding a comet nobody has catalogued needs the opposite direction: start from a field, find what moves, and ask which movers look like comets. The field analysis' tracklets (ADR-0048) only cover straight lines inside one visit faster than ~2"/h with detections at least 15 min apart, and nothing ranks them by how comet-like they are. SPHEREx makes this harder and easier at once: it sees a field in ~20 short passes (often ~7 min) spread over 10-50 days, from a low orbit whose 98-min wobble shifts an object at 1 au by 9.7", and every exposure is a different wavelength, so the movers come with a spectrum.

Decision

A new package spherex_core.comets and one job kind, h (comet search), on the field-job runner:

  • Movers on a fixed-position sequence: the field analysis' tracklets (reused from a finished field job or detected again), pass tracklets (>= 4 detections at >= 3 times inside one pass of minutes, 2-300"/h) and slow links over days. Slow links use group maps (each pass's static-sky subtracted exposures combined, the per-visit median removed) and the model x(t) = x0 + v dt + w B(t), where B is the apparent displacement of an object at 1 au from the observer's heliocentric motion (Earth from astropy, SPHEREx's own orbit from Horizons, cached) and w = 1/D; pairs vote in a Hough space, fits grow by prediction, and a link needs >= 4 passes and a significant motion. Fainter slow movers come from a parallax-aware shift-and-stack over (vx, vy, w) inside 6-day windows, capped at 8,000 trials per window and 60,000 per visit, with a threshold on the best-trial map's own spread.
  • Static sky for every exposure is the median co-add of the other visits (a mover is elsewhere then), with bright stars masked as in the field analysis.
  • Coma test: the object's stamps, shifted onto its track, are co-added and compared with field stars measured the same way (single exposures, same detectors, the object's flux per detector setting the mix). Size (Gaussian-weighted second moment) and concentration (annulus / core), with bootstrap errors over exposures and stars, a 5 % floor, the track's jitter and the different bilinear-interpolation smoothing (f (1 - f) per axis) corrected. Tail: the first azimuthal moment in units of the core flux, compared with the projected anti-solar direction (independent of distance) and the trail direction.
  • Spectral signature: forced photometry along the track at each exposure's wavelength; CO2 4.26, CO 4.67, H2O 2.7 um bands and 3 um ice with the band fits of ADR-0060; a 4-5 um reflectance excess as thermal context.
  • Orbit classes: digest2-style ranging over distance and range rate with coarse population densities in (a, e, i), the Delaunay phase-space Jacobian and a detectability weight; constrained by the distance when an ephemeris (co-moving) or a parallax fit gives one.
  • Comet-likeness 0-100 = logistic(logit P_orbit + morphology + spectrum), hand-weighted; the motion alone is capped at even odds, and spectral evidence counts only for a source detected along its track.
  • Identification of the best 12 measured candidates against JPL sb_ident (asteroids and comets), SPHEREx-centric at each candidate's first and last time, interactive timeouts and a 150 s budget; ADES rows (station 288, follow-up data, not a submission) for the measured candidates.
  • On a co-moving sequence the same tests run on its own object per visit ("is it a comet?"), which is also how we validate the score.
  • Known-comet watch: JPL SBDB comets with perihelion within 2 years, two-body positions and predicted magnitudes now; a background refresh (one at a time across workers) follows the pinned comets (3I, C/2025 A6, 29P by default) and the brightest others through MOST, a pinned prewarm-priority co-moving build, the comet analysis and the object check, and keeps per comet Afrho at 10^4 km, CO2 S/N, the activity slope and outburst flags (a visit >= 2x above the power law of the others at >= 3 sigma).
  • API POST /api/sequences/{id}/comet-search, GET /api/comets/watch, POST /api/comets/watch/refresh; MCP tools comet_search, comet_candidates, comet_watch; a "Comet hunt" rail panel in Explore and Research.

Each object check takes 1-3 s. Field hunts: across 5 fixed fields (40 measured movers) none scored as comet-like; before the crowded-field fixes in this ADR the crowded field near C/2025 A6's track gave 13 false positives; 97 Klotho was identified as a known asteroid (score 1.0). Fixed 8′ fields around single ~7-minute passes of C/2025 A6 and 3I/ATLAS missed both comets (0 of 2), so the hunt does not yet find fast comets from one pass. Each field takes 18-137 s, 12-60 s of it the JPL lookup. Crowded low-latitude fields remain the weak spot (see Consequences).

Consequences

  • Known comets separate cleanly from asteroids when the object is measured on a co-moving sequence: coma, tail alignment and CO2 agree, and point-like asteroids score near zero.
  • Field hunts are the noisy part. In crowded fields (|b| < 10 deg) static-sky residuals line up by chance; pass tracklets are therefore limited to 300"/h, need four detections at three times and confirmation by forced photometry along the track (co-added S/N >= 5), and the orbit term alone cannot make a candidate comet-like. A trained real/bogus model for pass tracklets and slow links is the next step.
  • A comet that crosses a fixed field within one ~7-min pass gives only a handful of frames: too few for a morphology verdict (6 are required), so it ranks on its spectrum and motion; the co-moving check is the way to confirm it.
  • The orbit-class prior is crude and hand-set; numbers are rankings. The watch's SBDB positions are two-body and good to about a degree; the co-moving builds use MOST/Horizons ephemerides.
  • Cost: a comet search is a field job (cancelled when nobody polls); slow linking and the stack search are capped; the watch builds at prewarm priority and never displaces interactive users.
