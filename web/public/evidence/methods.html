Methods

How a sequence is built, with the parameters the code uses today, what we have validated, and what we know is still weak. The reasoning behind each choice is in the ADRs; the data themselves are described in data.md.

Inputs

A request gives a target and processing options:

Parameter Default Meaning
ra, dec — ICRS position in degrees at ref_epoch
pmra, pmdec 0 proper motion, mas/yr (pmra includes cos δ)
ref_epoch 2000.0 Julian year of the position
fov_arcmin 6 field of view, 1–30 arcmin
gap_days 20 a gap longer than this starts a new epoch
max_epoch_days 45 an epoch never spans more than this

Pipeline

1. Search

Propagate the target to 2026.0 and query our local index of SPHEREx Level 2 files (a mirror of the archive's file catalogue, re-synced every hour; ADR-0057) for every science image whose footprint contains that position in the configured collections. IRSA TAP and SIA are fallbacks. Keep one image per (observation id, detector), preferring DR1 over QR3 (ADR-0003). Results are cached for 12 hours.

2. Output grid

Centre the grid on the target's position at the median MJD of its images. TAN projection, north up, east left, 6.15″ pixels, n = ceil(FOV / 6.15″) made odd (minimum 9); 6′ gives 59 × 59 pixels (ADR-0006).

3. Read cutouts

For each image, read a square of 2h + 1 native pixels around the grid centre, with h = ceil(n / 2 × 1.42) + 3 (45 for a 6′ field): IMAGE, FLAGS and VARIANCE.

  • Where from. First IRSA's cutout service, which cuts the window on its side: one request, ~160 KB at 6′, with at most 8 requests open per build. When no request slot is free, or the service is busy, slow or answers with something unusable, the image is read from S3 with HTTP range requests (FLAGS RICE tiles decoded on our side). Both give the same pixels (ADR-0002, ADR-0038).
  • What is kept. Each file's rows are cached in one chunk, with the columns of a 30′ field, so a wider or narrower field later reads only rows it does not hold yet. VARIANCE is read only within 22 px of the target, the farthest any measurement looks; elsewhere it is NaN (ADR-0037).
  • How. The images are read in batches of 2–16 on the process pool, each worker reading and processing its own (step 4), with 64 images in flight over the whole build. The epoch the viewer opens on is read first, and a preview of the finished epochs is published while the rest is read (ADR-0039).
  • Tables. The detector's wavelength table (WCS-WAVE) and effective-PSF table (EPSF, 441 zones) are read once per detector from S3 and cached (ADR-0016).

4. Per exposure (process pool)

  1. Mask. Set pixels with any image-mask bit to NaN: TRANSIENT, SUR_ERROR, NONFUNC, MISSING_DATA, HOT, COLD, PERSIST, OUTLIER, STREAK, SNOWBALL (ADR-0018).
  2. Background. Subtract the sigma-clipped median (3σ, 5 iterations) of the remaining pixels; keep the clipped standard deviation as the exposure's noise rms.
  3. Align. Map grid pixels to cutout pixels through the exposure WCS (SIP included) evaluated on a 9 × 9 control grid and a cubic spline. Resample bilinearly with NaN-aware weights (data × valid and valid resampled separately, output NaN below half valid weight) so masked areas do not grow; resample flags nearest-neighbour. The exposure is also aligned without masking (aligned_raw) for filling co-add holes (ADR-0017).
  4. Photometry at the target's position at MJD-AVG (ADR-0010, ADR-0016): - ePSF fit (primary, method: "psf"). The ePSF of the detector zone nearest the cutout centre (33 × 33, oversampled 5×) is evaluated with a cubic spline at the target's sub-pixel position and normalised to unit sum at that phase. Flux and a constant background are fitted by weighted linear least squares (weights 1/VARIANCE) on pixels within 5 px that are neither saturated (OVERFLOW, NONLINEAR) nor masked. A fit is made if the usable pixels hold ≥ 2 % of the PSF; npix, reduced chi2 and psf_weight (PSF fraction in the fitted pixels) are reported; saturated is set when a saturated pixel lies within 2 px. - Aperture (secondary, flux_ap_mjy). r = max(1.5 px, FWHM) with FWHM = PSF_FWHM / √OMEGA_MEDIAN; background: sigma-clipped (3σ) median in an annulus from max(4 px, 2.5 × FWHM) to 3 px further out; exact-overlap sum with Gaussian aperture correction 1/(1 − exp(−r²/2σ²)). It becomes the primary value (method: "aperture") when no fit is possible. - Radial profile for rings. Background-subtracted sums, variances and pixel counts in 1-px annuli out to 16 px (local background: median of usable pixels at 16–21 px, else the frame background), and the radius of the saturated core: the blob of OVERFLOW/NONLINEAR/TRANSIENT/SUR_ERROR/BLOOM pixels touching the source. PERSIST is left out of the core because persistence blobs from earlier exposures are not centred on the star (up to ~200 px around Barnard's Star in D3) (ADR-0020). - Errors. From VARIANCE, with a 3 % systematic floor added in quadrature. - Units and wavelength. MJy/sr × OMEGA_MEDIAN → mJy, AB magnitude; wavelength and bandwidth from the detector's WCS-WAVE table at the target pixel, bilinear in raw pixel coordinates. - flagged. For a fit: usable PSF weight < 20 %, more than 20 % of the PSF weight on masked pixels, or GHOST/BLOOM/CROSSTALK on a fitted pixel. For an aperture value: any photometry-bad bit in the aperture (image mask, OVERFLOW, NONLINEAR, GHOST, BLOOM, CROSSTALK; HALO is informational) or more than 20 % of it masked (ADR-0018).

5. Ring photometry for saturated detectors

A detector switches to rings when at least half of its exposures are saturated (or failed with a bad-pixel blob over the source). All its exposures then use one ring, from 1 px outside the 95th percentile of the saturated-core radii, 3 px wide (capped at 16 px); masked pixels in the ring are filled with their annulus mean, and exposures whose ring is cut by the cutout edge or has under 60 % usable pixels lose their photometry instead of mixing methods. These records have method: "ring", relative: true, ring_r_in, ring_r_out and no AB magnitude: the flux is the ring's share of the light, comparable only within one detector and wavelength. Divided by the median of their wavelength bin, as the web client does, they give a relative light curve. The chosen rings are in processing.rings (ADR-0020).

6. Epochs and co-adds

Sort exposures by time; start a new epoch after a gap > gap_days or when the epoch spans max_epoch_days. Then fold every epoch with fewer than 20 exposures into its nearest neighbour if they are less than 60 days apart; epochs still below 20 exposures are kept with partial: true (ADR-0019). For each epoch and detector: with ≥ 4 exposures, reject pixels more than 5 × max(1.4826 × MAD, median rms) from the per-pixel median; then take the mean weighted by 1/rms² per exposure. Pixels that no clean exposure covered (coverage 0, typically saturated star cores whose pixels also carry masked bits) are filled with the median of the unmasked values and get pipeline flag bit 31 FILLED. Also record the number of contributing exposures per pixel and the bitwise OR of flags (ADR-0007, ADR-0017).

7. Outputs

coadds.f32, coverage.u16, flags.u32 (FLAGS bits plus pipeline bit 31 FILLED), exposures.f16 (if ≤ 80 million voxels) and meta.json with the grid, epochs (dates, partial, exposures, wavelength range and filled pixels per detector), every exposure (date, detector, wavelength, noise, flag counts, predicted target pixel), photometry (method, flux_mjy, err_mjy, saturated, npix, chi2, psf_weight, flux_ap_mjy, err_ap_mjy, relative, ring_r_in, ring_r_out, flagged, …), flag definitions (pipeline: true for bit 31), the processing constants (processing), the predicted target track per epoch, and timing and transfer statistics (ADR-0005).

  • Reader correctness. For a DR1 file, the range reader's IMAGE, VARIANCE and FLAGS cutouts are identical to astropy's section reads (backend/tests/test_reader_network.py).
  • Wavelengths. Our WCS-WAVE interpolation equals astropy's FITS -TAB result to a relative 10⁻⁶ at four test pixels, once the SIP keywords are removed from the header (see the gotcha in data.md).

Known limitations

  • Heavily saturated stars. The ePSF covers only ±3.3 px and holds ~97 % of the flux in the central 3 × 3 pixels, so stars whose saturated core is larger than that (R Draconis, Barnard's Star, RW Aur) get ring photometry instead: relative fluxes only, no calibrated spectrum. Ring fluxes depend on the wing profile at 4–15 px, which changes with the detector position, so constant stars scatter by ~5 % per exposure and per-epoch medians can be off by up to ~14 % in some detectors (Barnard's Star D2/D3); amplitudes below ~15 % are not reliable with this method.
  • Blends. With 6.15″ pixels, neighbours within about 10″ contaminate both the ePSF fit (single source, fixed position) and the aperture. WISE 0855 passes 8″ from a G = 16.3 star in 2025–26, which dominates D1–D4 at its position.
  • Absolute flux scale rests on the ePSF normalisation; one field star agrees with 2MASS and WISE to within 8 %, more stars are needed for a proper calibration check.
  • Mixed wavelengths in co-adds. A detector co-add averages up to 17 channels; epochs that miss channels can change the apparent brightness of strongly coloured sources.
  • Filled co-add pixels (FILLED) come from flagged pixels: fine for display, not for measurement.
  • Parallax ignored in position propagation (0.55″ for Barnard's Star).
  • Fixed flag policy; users can display any flag but cannot yet change which bits are masked.
  • Running jobs are not persistent; a restart loses in-flight builds (finished results stay on disk).

Analyses on a built sequence

  • Photometry of any position (ADR-0027): the pipeline's measurements on the cached native cutouts, at a new position with optional proper motion, PSF zone nearest to that position. Re-measuring the targets reproduces the build's fluxes exactly for Barnard's Star (357 of 358 exposures) and with a median difference of zero for ε Indi B (maximum 6 %, expected where the PSF zone nearest the source differs from the one nearest the cutout centre that the build used). 11–14 s for 358–458 exposures in a cold script, no downloads.
  • Proper motion (ADR-0028): flux-weighted centroids per epoch and detector, a straight-line fit with per-detector zero points. ε Indi B 3628 ± 305 / −2526 ± 260 mas/yr (Gaia 3982 / −2467); Barnard's Star −1316 ± 325 / 9557 ± 385 (Gaia −802 / 10362); three static Gaia stars −98 … +57 mas/yr. Precision is about 300 mas/yr with 3–4 visits; per-detector centroids of bright, saturated stars scatter by ~0.5″.
  • Periodograms (ADR-0029): Lomb–Scargle on the client-identical relative light curve. R Dra 235.7 d (standard) and 246.9 d (multiband) against ~245 d; RW Aur's three visits give the visit spacing (131 d), not a period.

Calibration checks (2026-11-14)

  • Wavelengths. WCS-WAVE (the 9 × 9 lookup we use) against the EPSF table's CWAVE at all 441 zone centres: median offset +0.02–0.04 % (D1–D4) and +0.14–0.16 % (D5–D6), maximum 0.19 % — a fifth of a resolution element or less. Bandwidths agree in the median (−0.04 %) but differ by up to 4 % (95th percentile) in some zones. No change needed.
  • Pixel solid angle. The pixel area from the WCS (with SIP) is 0.03 % below OMEGA_MEDIAN at the detector centre and 0.9–1.3 % away from it at the corners and edges, so using OMEGA_MEDIAN for every pixel biases fluxes by up to ~1.3 % near the edges. Proposed (needs an algorithm version bump): compute the solid angle at the target pixel from the WCS Jacobian.

Archival epochs and solar-system objects

Archival epochs (ADR-0023)

  1. CAOM TAP search around the grid centre: 2MASS and AllWISE images that intersect the field, NEOWISE-R frames that contain the centre (spherex_core/archival/sources.py).
  2. Pixels: AllWISE Atlas tiles and NEOWISE-R frames by range reads from s3://nasa-irsa-wise (rows covering the field only); 2MASS images downloaded whole (gzip, ~1.5 MB).
  3. Calibration to MJy/sr: DN × F0 × 10^(−0.4 MAGZP) / Ω_pixel, Vega zero points 1594 / 1024 / 666.7 Jy (J/H/Ks) and 309.54 / 171.79 Jy (W1/W2).
  4. Background: sigma-clipped median subtracted per image.
  5. Smoothing to the SPHEREx scale: NaN-aware Gaussian, σ = 0.35 × (6.15″ / native pixel) native pixels (2.2 px for 2MASS, 1.6 for AllWISE, 0.8 for NEOWISE); then bilinear resampling onto the sequence grid.
  6. Epochs: 2MASS images within 3 days mosaicked (mean), all AllWISE tiles mosaicked (mean), NEOWISE single exposures median-combined per visit (gap > 20 days or span > 30 days starts a new visit; ≥ 3 frames).

Static sky, comets, asteroids, unknown movers (ADR-0060)

Static sky ("different background, same comet"). A co-moving build with static_sky gives every exposure a template made of SPHEREx's own images of the same patch: same detector, wavelength within 1.5 resolution elements (from the footprint corners and the detector's wavelength map, re-measured after alignment), taken while the object was more than the grid half-diagonal plus a coma margin away (ephemeris interpolated inside a visit, extrapolated up to 30 days along it). Up to four candidates are read; the template is their median (two or more within 0.5 dλ), a linear interpolation in wavelength, or the nearest one. The clean exposure is aligned − template − outer offset; pixels where the template is bright enough that the measured residual fraction ε would leave more than 3σ (template > max(3, 3/ε) σ) are masked. The clean stacks are the normal co-adds of the clean exposures.

Asteroids. Fluxes from the ePSF photometry, or clean apertures (2 FWHM) scaled to it per detector; saturated detectors re-measured in their ring on the clean exposures (relative, light curve only). Reduced reflectance magnitudes at light-time corrected times; per visit a quadratic in wavelength per detector, a phase slope and a Fourier series; Δχ² summed over visits and zoomed at the peak. One brightness maximum per periodogram cycle (topographic prominence ≥ 10 %) means the peak is the second harmonic: P = 2 × peak, confirmed when the odd harmonics are significant. period.confidence is high when two or more visits peak on the joint period and it is not within 3 % of a harmonic of SPHEREx's 98.6-min orbit.

Unknown movers. Fixed-position sequences only: the field analysis' moving-object detection and JPL identification (or a finished field job), then every tracklet without a JPL match listed with its evidence and a tier (strong: high quality, ≥ 4 detections, detector pair confirmed ≥ 50 %, real/bogus ≥ 0.6), one step lower when a tracklet of another visit lies within 6″ (a real mover is elsewhere months later). Identification failures make tracklets "unverified". The ADES export (station 288) writes the strong ones; it is follow-up data for MPChecker / NEOCP checks, not a discovery claim.

Comet discovery (ADR-0063)

The comet search (spherex_core.comets) finds movers in a fixed field (field-analysis tracklets, single-pass tracklets, slow links over days with SPHEREx's parallax x(t) = x0 + v dt + B(t)/D, and a capped shift-and-stack), measures each along its track in exposures minus the other visits' static sky, and ranks them by a 0-100 comet-likeness: coma extent and tail against field stars built the same way (bootstrap errors, interpolation and jitter corrected), CO2 / CO / H2O bands from the per-exposure spectrum, and a digest2-style orbit-class share (ranging with population densities). On a co-moving sequence it checks the object itself: 3I/ATLAS 99.9, C/2025 A6 100, 433 Eros <= 1, 16 Psyche 0.1 (ADR-0063 has the table). Scores are hand-weighted rankings, not probabilities.

Spectral analysis

Code: spherex_core/spectral/ (ADRs 0040–0044); endpoints in api.

  1. Binning. Per-exposure photometry is combined on a fixed grid of 102 bins (17 geometric bins per detector, width ≈ λ/R). Inverse-variance mean after a 4σ robust cut; error = max(formal, scatter/√n). Absolute spectra use calibrated, unflagged points; ring points of saturated stars are binned as relative spectra. Built overall and per epoch.
  2. Features. 16 curated features (H₂O 1.4/1.9/2.7, CH₄ 1.65/2.2/3.3, CO 2.3/4.6, CO₂ 4.26, H₂O ice 3.05, CO₂ ice 4.27, CO ice 4.67, PAH 3.3, Paα, Brγ, Brα) measured against power-law side windows (one-sided ones carried with the local continuum slope), 2 % systematic per window; depth, EW, significance for each. Flux-ratio indices (methane 1.6/2.2/3.3 µm, water 1.4/1.9 µm, CO, 4 µm peak/J, K/4.5 µm). Automatic detection against a log-log spline pseudo-continuum with asymmetric clipping, flagged unreliable when more than a quarter of the bins sit > 3σ below it (cool dwarfs).
  3. Classification. Template χ² (Pickles 131 stars + SpeX standards M0–T9, integrated onto the grid, fitted below 2.45 µm with a 3 % floor; relative probabilities after inflating errors to χ²/dof = 1), blackbody + extinction grid (Indebetouw 2005), IR index α over 2–5 µm, IR excess at 3–3.8 and 3.8–5 µm against the ≤ 2.45 µm blackbody, methane index; combined into a label and a family (star, late M dwarf, brown dwarf, composite, YSO candidate).
  4. Synthetic photometry. 2MASS J/H/Ks and WISE W1/W2 from the spectrum with SVO response curves and Vega zero points (W2 extended past 5 µm with a power law), compared with matched 2MASS/CatWISE magnitudes.
  5. Variability. Ratio spectra of each epoch against a reference epoch with a 3 % systematic per bin, S/N ≥ 3 in both, per-detector weighted ratios and significance; usable for ring photometry.

Automated field analysis

spherex_core.field searches every source of a built sequence (ADR-0045 to ADR-0052).

  1. Sources (ADR-0046): peaks above S/N 5 in the all-epoch mean and S/N 6 per epoch, per detector, merged within 2 px; peaks in the halo or saturated footprint of a bright star are folded into it; fluxes per detector from the all-epoch mean (r = 2 px, nominal 1/0.80 aperture correction; approximate).
  2. Cross-match: Gaia DR3, CatWISE2020, 2MASS moved to the sequence's mean epoch, nearest within 1.5 px.
  3. Movers (ADR-0047): blind ADR-0026 motion fits for unblended sources with S/N ≥ 8; ≥ 4σ, ≥ 500 mas/yr, < 15″/yr and at least two agreeing detectors; compared with Gaia/CatWISE. Shift-and-stack over 1–15″/yr on static-subtracted epoch maps for faint movers.
  4. Moving Object Detection (ADR-0048): exposure-minus-co-add S/N maps, simultaneous-pair check, real/bogus (ADR-0051), tracklets of ≥ 3 detections per visit, identification against known objects seen from SPHEREx (ADR-0049); Track & Stack and synthetic tracking within a visit (ADR-0050); verification with free-position ePSF fits and ADES export for station 288 (ADR-0052).
  5. Variability: aperture (or ring outside a saturated core) photometry of every source in every aligned exposure, normalised per wavelength bin; reduced χ² with a 3 % floor, Stetson J, amplitude between epoch medians; candidates need ≥ 20 points, amplitude ≥ 10 % (≥ 20 % for rings) and χ² ≥ 4; periodograms for the top five when there are ≥ 5 epochs.
  6. Colours: red excess (D5 + D6 vs D1–D3) per source and as a pixel map where stars cancel (catches a cold dwarf blended with a bluer star), plus an isolation-forest anomaly score on adjacent-detector flux ratios.

Known-object identification (ADR-0054) uses SPHEREx-centric positions queried at each tracklet's first and last detection time and interpolated between them. sb_ident's rates are instantaneous rates seen from SPHEREx and swing by about ±15″/h over the 98-min orbit for an object 2.4 AU away (Klotho's Dec rate read −11.7″/h and +14″/h half an orbit apart while it drifted south at 2″/h), so they are not used to propagate positions.

Point analysis ("click a point → Analyze")

POST /api/sequences/{id}/analyze runs these steps for one position (ADR-0053). Since ADR-0056 they run concurrently within a 60 s deadline in interactive network mode (15 s timeouts, per-service circuit breakers, stale catalogue cache when a service is down): identification, known objects, tracklets and motion start at once; photometry waits up to 8 s for a catalogue counterpart to follow, then spectrum, periodogram and colours use its records. Photometry reads only the sequence's own cutout cache (never an IRSA search) and runs in the process pool; motion starts at the measured target's position at the sequence's mean epoch, with its catalogue motion as prior.

  1. Identification: Gaia DR3 / CatWISE2020 / 2MASS / SIMBAD cones (radius at each catalogue's epoch wide enough for 10″/yr movers), every source moved to the sequence's mean epoch; the nearest three within 15″, the counterpart if ≤ 4″, and each match's position at every epoch.
  2. Photometry: re-measure in every exposure (ePSF / aperture / ring, as the pipeline). With no motion given, the analysis follows a Gaia (else SIMBAD) counterpart's motion, so fast movers are measured where they are.
  3. Spectrum: binned spectrum, features and indices, template / blackbody classification and ratio spectra between epochs (the spectral module).
  4. Motion: blind proper-motion fit from per-epoch co-add centroids (no Gaia prior, so it can be compared).
  5. Periodogram: Lomb–Scargle of the point's photometry, normalised per wavelength bin.
  6. Colours: weighted mean flux density at 3.8–5 µm against 0.75–2.4 µm; red excess when the long-wavelength S/N ≥ 5 and the ratio ≥ 0.8 or the short-wavelength S/N < 3.
  7. Known objects: sb_ident from SPHEREx at each epoch's mid-time (≤ 8 epochs); objects within 30″.
  8. Tracklets: candidates within 20″ from a finished field analysis of the sequence (none are searched here); unidentified ones are re-identified against known objects with bracketed positions. A mover at the point suppresses the proper-motion claim (its centroid shift is not a proper motion); without Gaia a motion needs ≥ 5σ to be called a candidate.
  9. Verdict: explicit rules turn the slots into findings with a confidence and a novelty contribution; novelty = 0.1 + Σ contributions, clipped to [0, 1]. Unexplained motion (+0.55), an unidentified high-quality intra-visit mover (+0.5), red excess without Gaia (+0.35) and brown-dwarf or composite spectra raise it; SIMBAD (−0.3), Gaia (−0.15) or a known asteroid (−0.1) lower it.

Each step is independent: a failing upstream service leaves {"error"} in its slot and the rest still runs.

Long-baseline light curves (ADR-0059)

POST /api/sequences/{id}/long-baseline measures the target, or any position in the field, on the archival frames themselves. SPHEREx is then put on the same band scales, and the 1997–2026 light curve is analysed (spherex_core.longbaseline).

  1. Frames. Native-pixel windows are read around the target's 2018 position, with a 150″ radius (the request's field_radius, 60–360″; wider for fast movers), from every 2MASS Atlas image, the AllWISE Atlas tile and up to 12 NEOWISE L1b frames per visit and band. NEOWISE windows carry the msk-1b mask: saturation bits 10–18 and the bad-pixel bits. Windows are cached, so a repeat reads nothing.
  2. Photometry per frame, at the position moved to the frame's MJD: - Aperture. The survey's standard aperture (WISE 8.25″, 2MASS 4″) with a 3σ-clipped annulus (WISE 50–70″, 2MASS 14–20″), corrected to the catalogue scale with the measured enclosed energy. Bad pixels in the aperture take the mean of the good pixels at the same radius; frames with more than 10% of the PSF weight masked are flagged. - Saturated cores. When at least half of a band's frames saturate at the source, every frame is measured in one ring outside the core and scaled by the ring's enclosed-energy fraction. The ring starts 1 px outside the 95th percentile of the core radii and is 4 px wide in NEOWISE and 2MASS, 8 px in AllWISE. A NEOWISE core is a pixel with saturation bits 10–18, or a NaN that no bad-pixel bit explains (L1b sets dead and noisy pixels to NaN too). AllWISE Atlas tiles have no mask and their co-added cores are only partly NaN, so an AllWISE ring starts no closer than the NEOWISE ring of the same band. - Combining. Frames are combined per NEOWISE visit (a new visit after a 20 d gap), per 2MASS night and once for AllWISE: a 3σ-clipped weighted mean whose error is at least the scatter error, plus a floor of 1.5% (aperture) or 3% (ring). - 2MASS saturation. Bands saturated in 2MASS use the PSC magnitude (read-1 photometry) when the PSC has one.
  3. Enclosed energy. Measured with analysis/validation/long_baseline_calibration.py on the five validation fields (61 2MASS, 31 W1 and 22 W2 stars). Each frame's growth curve of an isolated, unsaturated star is divided by the star's catalogue flux in that frame's DN, minus the expected flux of faint field sources (far-annulus mean − median per pixel). The aperture corrections are:

Beyond about 10 px (NEOWISE) or 30 px (AllWISE) the curves exceed 1 by up to 10%: PSF wings beyond the catalogue's profile-fit normalisation, plus residual neighbour light. The ring scale is therefore approximate. Two isolated saturated AllWISE stars (W1 5.8 and 6.3, rings at 6–10 px) give ring − catalogue offsets of −0.13 mag in W1 and +0.47 mag in W2, consistent between the two stars to 0.02 mag. 4. Cross-calibration. Up to 16 field stars within field_radius − 10″ (140″ by default) are measured exactly like the target, on the frames and in every SPHEREx exposure. They are CatWISE2020 stars (AllWISE from a VizieR mirror when IRSA fails), isolated (no neighbour within 16″ brighter than W1 + 2.5), with W1 9–14 and W2 7.5–13.5. - Frames → catalogue (median and robust scatter per survey and band): applied to aperture points with ≥ 4 stars. - SPHEREx synthetic → NEOWISE mean (W1/W2) or PSC (J/H/Ks): uses stars constant in both, applied with ≥ 3 stars, else the default. 5. Synthetic bands. Each SPHEREx time bin (epochs cut into ≤ 15-day chunks) is a binned spectrum, integrated as

   ⟨Fν⟩ = ∫Fν R/λ / ∫R/λ

with SVO responses, log-linear interpolation and power-law extrapolation, and ≥ 75% of the photon weight covered. The error comes from the band's Jacobian with respect to each spectral bin, plus a 2% floor. Ring targets give relative synthetic points. D1–D6 per-bin fluxes are the bin over the all-epoch spectrum, times the detector's mean flux. 6. Analysis. - Period search. Multiband generalised Lomb–Scargle over W1, W2 and D1–D6, with a free zero point per offset group and per-band sinusoids. The grid runs from 50 d to baseline/1.5 at 10 samples per peak. - Significance. FAP from an F-test times the number of independent frequencies; σ_P from the χ² curvature with χ²/dof rescaled to 1. Significant when FAP < 10⁻³ and the power is ≥ 0.3. - Final model with 2 harmonics, giving peak-to-peak amplitudes per band and phase-folded points. - From the residuals: - trends over ≥ 3 yr; - era means per survey; - events: runs of ≥ 2 archival visits or ≥ 3 SPHEREx bins beyond max(3σ, 0.1 mag), or one point confirmed within 5 d by an independent survey or archival band (SPHEREx bands never confirm each other). - Cadence aliases. NEOWISE alternates its scan direction every visit, so a neighbour's diffraction spikes, ghosts and latents come and go with a 1-yr pattern that the 6-monthly sampling aliases to 365.25/k d. A best period within half the NEOWISE frequency resolution of 365.25/k d (k = 1–7) is reported with cadence_alias: k and is not significant. - Verdict. "Variable" needs, in one band of W1/W2, a constant-model χ²/dof ≥ 5 and a 5–95% range ≥ max(0.1 mag, 5 median errors); pure noise gives a range of about 3.3 errors. The other band, when it has ≥ 8 points, must not be flat (χ²/dof ≥ 2), and the variability must not be the scan pattern (a cadence alias as best period at power ≥ 0.5). "Periodic" also needs a significant period, and a period ≥ 100 d makes it a long-period variable. 7. Field search (/long-period-search). Up to 150 CatWISE sources with W1 ≤ 15 within radius_arcsec − 10″ (140″ by default) of the field centre are measured on the same NEOWISE/AllWISE frames, normalised per NEOWISE visit by the ensemble of unsaturated sources with S/N > 20. SPHEREx adds relative aperture points on the epoch co-adds. Each source goes through the same model, and sources are ranked by log χ²/dof + 2 × range (+ 1 + power when periodic). Contaminated sources are measured but listed after the clean ones and left out of the summary: an AllWISE W1/W2 contamination flag (ccf: halo, diffraction spike, ghost, latent); within 20″ (60″ for a saturated target) of a moving sequence target's 1997–2027 track, since CatWISE splits fast movers into several entries; or a median NEOWISE W1 more than 0.5 mag off the catalogue (the aperture also measures a neighbour). In the validation they carry almost all spurious variability.

Limitations

  • Ring scale. The absolute scale of ring points (heavily saturated stars) rests on the enclosed-energy curve at 5–20 px, which is uncertain at the 0.1–0.5 mag level. The analysis fits ring groups' zero points, so periods, amplitudes and shapes do not depend on it. Era-to-era comparisons of an all-ring star do: one ring per survey means a different radius in each survey. Rings far out in the wings (< 0.5% of the PSF, scale_uncertain) can be off by a magnitude or more (V1968 Cyg W2).
  • Blends. WISE's 6″ PSF blends close pairs (RW Aur A+B at 1.4″). The aperture photometry is not deblended, and neighbours within the 8.25″ aperture add their flux.
  • Calibration stars. Fields at high latitude or around very bright stars have few isolated catalogue stars within field_radius − 10″. R Dra has none even at 360″ (CatWISE is nearly empty around it). Its SPHEREx points then use the default offsets (±0.02 mag) and its frames the enclosed-energy correction.
  • Sampling. NEOWISE visits every ~6 months alias periods near 1 yr and 6 months. SPHEREx bins and AllWISE / 2MASS points break the aliases only when the period is significant on its own. A real period within the resolution of 365.25/k d is reported as a cadence alias, not as significant. Periods < 50 d are not searched.
  • Image lists need IRSA. Finding new archival frames uses IRSA's CAOM search (cached per field for a week). The catalogues fall back to VizieR mirrors and Gaia DR3, but with IRSA down a field without cached image lists cannot get a long-baseline light curve.
